### Overview & Mindset
I operate under the guiding principle of wealth preservation over rapid accumulation. My psychological profile is characterized by loss aversion; for me, the pain of a portfolio drawdown significantly outweighs the pleasure of an equivalent gain. I view the stock market as a vehicle for protecting my purchasing power against inflation and generating steady, reliable income rather than a casino for speculative bets. Peace of mind is my primary ROI.

### Risk Tolerance
I exhibit a low risk appetite. I actively avoid high volatility, unproven business models, and companies with excessive leverage or irregular earnings histories. My "sleep-well-at-night" factor is paramount, meaning I will willingly sacrifice potential alpha to ensure my principal remains secure. I generally steer clear of small-cap stocks, IPOs, and cyclical industries during economic downturns.

### Investment Horizon
I maintain a long-term horizon, typically looking at windows of five to twenty years or longer. My rotation style is extremely low; I prefer a "buy and hold" strategy where portfolio turnover is minimized to reduce tax liabilities and transaction costs. I only exit positions when the fundamental thesis of a company permanently breaks or when rebalancing is strictly necessary.

### Decision-Making Style
My decisions are driven by historical data and fundamental solidity rather than future promises. I prioritize balance sheet strength, consistent dividend history, and established market dominance. I look for companies with wide economic moats and pricing power. My evaluation relies heavily on traditional valuation metrics, and I am skeptical of "adjusted" earnings, preferring GAAP profitability.

### Preferred Sectors & Assets
In the U.S. market, I gravitate toward defensive sectors that perform well regardless of the economic cycle. My key areas of interest include Consumer Staples, Utilities, and Healthcare. I favor "Dividend Aristocrats" and "Kings," blue-chip large-caps with decades of operational history, and high-grade corporate or treasury bonds.

### Information Sensitivity
I am highly sensitive to macroeconomic shifts that threaten income stability, such as rising inflation or interest rate hikes that make fixed income more attractive than equities. Specific corporate news regarding dividend cuts or suspensions is an immediate sell signal for me. I pay less attention to daily price fluctuations and more attention to quarterly earnings consistency.

### How to Tailor Your Analysis for Me
* Tone and Framing: Your analysis must be reassuring, prudent, and objective. Avoid hype; focus on stability and safety.
* Risk Disclosures: Highlight downside scenarios first. Explicitly detail debt levels and payout sustainability before telling me about growth potential.
* Metrics to Emphasize: Focus on Dividend Yield, Payout Ratio, Debt-to-Equity, Beta, and Free Cash Flow.
* Buying/Selling Cues: Advise me to buy on valuations dipping below historical averages (value traps notwithstanding) and to sell on dividend cuts or fundamental deterioration.
* Style of Recommendation: Be conservative. I prefer recommendations that prioritize "Hold" or "Accumulate" ratings over aggressive "Buy" calls.
