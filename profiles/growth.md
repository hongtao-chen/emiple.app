### Overview & Mindset
I am driven by the pursuit of capital appreciation and am motivated by the concept of future potential. I believe that stock prices ultimately follow the trajectory of revenue growth and innovation. Psychologically, I am optimistic and forward-looking, willing to endure short-term volatility and drawdowns if I believe the long-term investment thesis remains intact. I view the market as a mechanism to participate in the success of disruptive companies.

### Risk Tolerance
I maintain a moderate to high risk tolerance. I accept that high-growth companies often burn cash and may not be profitable today. I am comfortable with high valuations (high P/E ratios) as long as the growth rate justifies the premium. I actively avoid "melting ice cubes"—stagnant legacy companies—even if they appear cheap.

### Investment Horizon
I operate on a medium to long-term horizon, typically three to ten years. I understand that compounding takes time and that disruptive technologies do not mature overnight. My rotation style is moderate; I am willing to trim winners to manage portfolio weighting, but I generally let my winners run.

### Decision-Making Style
My decisions are based on the size of the opportunity and the quality of the execution. I analyze the Total Addressable Market (TAM), revenue growth rates, competitive advantages (moats), and the scalability of the business model. I am less concerned with current dividends and more concerned with how the company reinvests capital to expand market share.

### Preferred Sectors & Assets
I heavily favor the Technology and Consumer Discretionary sectors. Within the U.S. market, I look for leaders in Cloud Computing, Artificial Intelligence, Biotechnology, Fintech, and Green Energy. I prefer equities over bonds and am willing to invest in mid-cap and large-cap companies that are still in their expansion phase.

### Information Sensitivity
I am highly sensitive to earnings reports, specifically revenue guidance and user growth metrics. Regulatory news (antitrust) and interest rate changes (which affect the discount rate of future cash flows) significantly impact my sentiment. A slowing of growth velocity is my primary red flag.

### How to Tailor Your Analysis for Me
* Tone and Framing: The tone should be optimistic but analytical, focusing on the "story" and the path to profitability or dominance.
* Risk Disclosures: Focus on execution risk, competition, and valuation compression rather than daily volatility.
* Metrics to Emphasize: Highlight Year-over-Year (YoY) Revenue Growth, Gross Margins, TAM, PEG Ratio, and R&D spending.
* Buying/Selling Cues: Advise me to buy on dips caused by temporary market fear; advise me to sell when revenue growth decelerates significantly or the competitive moat breaches.
* Style of Recommendation: Be moderate to aggressive. Recommendations should highlight "Outperform" potential.
